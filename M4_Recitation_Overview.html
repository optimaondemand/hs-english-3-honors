THE MODULE 4 RECITATION
On the Air: Choose a Passage from the Year and Keep It Alive
In this quarter's novel, people who are forbidden books keep them the only way left: by learning them by heart and carrying them in their memory. Your fourth and final recitation does the same thing. You choose a passage from anywhere in your year of reading, you learn it by heart, and you speak it inside your own episode of The Living Books Broadcast, the module project, as the passage your broadcast exists to preserve.

🎤 WHAT THIS IS

Once each quarter you have memorized and performed a passage connected to that quarter's text: a speech from Hamlet, a passage of Douglass's oratory, a Victorian poem. This quarter the choice opens to the whole year, because the novel you are reading is about exactly this: what a person can carry when everything written down is taken away. The recitation is not a separate item this time. It is the heart of your broadcast, the segment in which the host stops arguing and simply speaks the thing worth saving. The same rules apply, and so does the same rubric.

🎯 HOW YOU PERFORM IT (READ CAREFULLY)

You recite from memory, with your eyes covered. Wear a blindfold, close your eyes, or turn away from any screen or page. This proves the words are truly memorized and frees you to perform rather than read. Two ways, and you choose one:

🎥 Option A: Record a video
Record yourself on any device you have (a phone is fine), eyes covered, then submit the video on the Week 8 On-Air Recitation page with a caption or transcript. Place the audio from that same recording inside your episode as its recitation segment.
💬 Option B: Live with your teacher
Your teacher may instead choose to hear the recitation live, also with eyes closed. If so, they will schedule it; you still record the passage separately, in audio, for the episode.
📜 WHERE YOUR PASSAGE MAY COME FROM (15–25 LINES, OR 110–185 WORDS OF PROSE)

Your passage is the thing your broadcast preserves, so choose it to fit the pitch you make in Week 2. Four sources are open to you:

1. The year's recitation menus
Any passage from the Module 1 (Hamlet), Module 2 (Douglass), or Module 3 (Victorian poems) menus, including one you already performed if you want to bring it to air with a year's more understanding.
2. Any public-domain text from the year
A continuous passage from Hamlet, the Federalist, Emerson, Douglass, or Jane Eyre that was not on a menu, cited by act and scene, paper number, or chapter.
3. Your independent-reading book
A passage from any book you read on your own this year, from your own copy, cited by chapter or page in that edition.
4. Fahrenheit 451 itself
A passage from your own copy of this quarter's novel, cited by Part. If the book about saving books is the book you would save, say so on air.
You choose in Week 6 and name the source and citation when you submit.
📊 HOW IT IS GRADED (20 POINTS)
Criteria Strong Developing Pts
Accuracy of text
7 pts		 From memory, eyes covered, faithful to the words and their order; slips are rare and self-recovered; the caption or transcript matches the source. Frequent errors, skipped lines, or long stalls; or eyes open / clearly reading. 7
Vocal delivery
7 pts		 Clear and controlled for a listener who cannot see you; pace, pause, and stress follow the sense; the passage is spoken to an audience, not recited at a wall. Rushed or flat; mechanical pauses; hard to follow by ear alone. 7
Interpretation
6 pts		 Delivery choices show real understanding of the passage's meaning and weight, and of why it is worth preserving. Recited correctly but with little sense of what the words mean or why they were chosen. 6
Total: 20 pts
