📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The book people in this quarter's novel do not skim each other. They listen the way you are about to learn to.
You are finishing your own episode this week, and your classmates are finishing theirs. Before you revise your own, learn to listen to someone else's, because a critical ear turned outward first is sharper when you turn it back on your own work. Today's reading tool has nothing to do with the page: it is a way of listening. A broadcast asks something different of an audience than a printed page does. You cannot flip back. You cannot reread a sentence while the next one plays. Good listening is a skill, and it is the skill your own listeners will use on you.

🎤 PART 1 · A WARM-UP BEFORE WE BEGIN
💭 QUICK CHECK-IN
Think of the last time you listened to a podcast, a speech, or an audio reading all the way through, with nothing else on screen. What made you keep listening rather than drift off?

Four things a disciplined listener listens for

A printed argument can be reread. A broadcast cannot, unless you deliberately pause and back it up, which most listeners will not do. That means a good listener works harder in the moment, and works for four things at once: the claim (what is this host arguing must survive, and why); the evidence (what sources and reasons back the claim, and are they credible); the delivery choices (pace, pause, emphasis, where the voice speeds up or slows down, and what each choice is doing to the argument); and what the preserved passage adds (the moment the host stops arguing and simply speaks the preserved passage, and what that moment does that the argument alone could not). None of these four is a grade for niceness. All four are questions about craft.

📋 PART 2 · THE LISTENER'S CARD (YOUR READING TOOL)

Use this card while any spoken piece plays, your own draft included. It is built to be filled in during a single listen, without pausing.

THE LISTENER'S CARD: FOUR BOXES, ONE LISTEN
The claim
In one sentence, what is this host saying must be preserved, and why.
The evidence
One source or reason named, and whether it held up.
The delivery
One moment the voice changed, and what the change did.
The preserved passage
What the preserved passage adds that the surrounding argument alone could not.
Fill one line per box during a single listen. Do not pause to perfect your notes; a listener's card is quick by design.

✍️ PART 4 · DO SOMETHING WITH IT: A FOUR-QUESTION LISTENING NOTE

Play your own current draft episode once, straight through, with the listener's card open. Then answer these four questions honestly, as if you were a stranger hearing it for the first time.

📓
READING RESPONSE
Write your four-question listening note on your own draft episode: (1) What did I understand, clearly and completely, on one listen? (2) Where was I lost, even for a moment, and what caused it? (3) What did the voice do in this recording that the written page could not have done? (4) What would I cut if I had to lose thirty seconds? Answer all four honestly; this note is what revision acts on.
🤖 WORKING WITH AI

Claude has not heard your recording and cannot judge your delivery, your pacing, or whether the preserved passage landed. The four-question listening note above is a listening skill, not a summarizing task; if you ask Claude to review your episode from the script alone, it can only guess at what a real ear would catch. Listen with your own ears first.
