📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The captain's car is barely out of the driveway before Montag is on his knees at the ventilator grille.
A novel can tell you who a character is by what he does when he thinks no one is looking. Today's reading tool is reading an object as an act: not what a thing is, but what the choice to keep it, hide it, or reveal it says about the person who made that choice. Montag has been hiding books for some time without telling Mildred, or fully admitting it to himself. The moment he shows her is the first time in the novel that keeping something becomes a decision made out loud, in front of another person, instead of a secret kept from everyone, including himself.

📚 PART 1 · A WARM-UP BEFORE WE BEGIN
💭 QUICK CHECK-IN
Think of something you have kept without telling anyone why, a photo, an object, a habit. What would it cost you to explain it out loud to someone else?

Reading an object as an act

Objects in fiction rarely just sit there. What a character keeps, and what he is willing to risk to keep it, is a kind of argument the character is making without necessarily saying so. Ask three questions of any kept object: what did it cost to keep (time, risk, effort); who else knows, and what changes when one more person does; and what does the keeper want, once the secret is out, that he could not want while it stayed hidden. A secret kept alone is private. A secret shown to one other person is a request, whether or not the character would call it that.

READING AN OBJECT AS AN ACT: THREE QUESTIONS
What did it cost to keep?
Time, risk, the constant fear of the Hound. Keeping is never free in this novel.
Who else knows, now?
A secret held alone and a secret shown to a spouse are not the same act, even if the object never moves.
What does the keeper want, now?
Once shown, a secret usually wants a response. What is Montag asking Mildred for, in showing her?
Keeping something and showing it are two different acts. Watch which one Montag does in this reading, and what changes between them.
📖 PART 2 · NOW READ: WHAT MONTAG HAS KEPT

Continue in Part One from the moment Beatty's car pulls away: Montag reveals to Mildred the books he has been hiding around the house, her horror at the risk, his insistence that they at least try to understand what is in them, and the two of them beginning to read together as squadrons of jet bombers roar overhead toward a war the news barely mentions. This finishes Part One. Watch for: how many books Montag has actually kept and where he has hidden them; what Mildred is more afraid of, the books themselves or being caught with them; and what Montag says he and Mildred need instead of the peace the house is built for.
