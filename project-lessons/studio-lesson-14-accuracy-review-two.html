1
What this milestone is
The second formal Accuracy Review
●
Your first Accuracy Review happened early in the year, on your original sources. This is the second, more exacting pass, now that your fine cut is locked. Every line you attribute to a text, every date, every claim about a person or an event, gets checked one more time against your Source and Line Log and, wherever possible, against the source itself. This is also the year's second lesson in not trusting an AI tool blindly: you ask Claude to flag places your captions might be wrong, then you, not Claude, decide what is true.
The medium. The review applies to the narrated slide deck or short video walkthrough, whichever form the work takes.

See one claim-checklist entry
A model, and a check
WHAT A CLAIM-CHECKLIST ENTRY LOOKS LIKE (A MODEL, NOT YOUR ANSWER)
Claim: narration states the schoolroom scene is set in 1849.
Source checked: Source and Line Log, entry 12, citing Douglass's Narrative, chapters 6 and 7.
Result: corrected. The log's own citation places the events earlier, in the mid-1820s to early 1830s. The 1849 date was a placeholder I never updated.
Fix: narration changed to: in his childhood. The source supports that without inventing a false precision.
Claude flag on this line: yes, flagged as a specific date with no citation attached. I checked it against the log and found the error myself.

Mark every claim
Your checklist, before checking
●
Read through your captions once, on paper or on screen, with a highlighter, real or digital. Mark every sentence that states a fact, a date, a name, or a line attributed to a source. That marked list is your claim checklist before you check a single one against your log.
List the claims you marked, one per line
Textual references if you adapted a dramatic work. Factual claims if you made a documentary.

●

Mark each one verified (it matches), corrected (you found an error and fixed it), or disclosed as license (a deliberate dramatic or artistic departure that your Accuracy Statement will name). 5
Write the Accuracy Statement
About 300 words
●
Write your Accuracy Statement, about 300 words. Name your sources. Disclose every deliberate departure from them and say why you made it. Describe what you changed as a result of this review. It travels with your published kit.
Your Accuracy Statement, about 300 words
Sources named. Every departure disclosed, with the reason. What changed after this review.
6
Ask Claude for flags, then verify
Every flag is a question, never an answer
●
Use Claude, then log it. Paste your caption text, text only, into Claude and ask it to flag likely anachronisms (a date, an object, or a phrase that does not belong to the period or setting) and unsupported claims (a fact stated with no source behind it). Treat every flag as a question, never an answer. Claude is guessing from pattern, not from your actual sources. It can flag something true, and it can miss something false. Check each flag against a real source yourself, then confirm, correct, or disclose it.
