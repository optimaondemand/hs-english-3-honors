What this milestone is
Finish the capture, then layer the sound
●
Milestone 10 captured the first half. Milestone 11 finishes the capture and adds what makes it a finished piece instead of raw footage: title cards, a visible list of your citations, and a sound plan carried out with ambience (the sound of the place), score (music), and effects (single sounds, like a door). Every sound must be royalty-free or made by you, and every one needs a license note: where it came from and what the license allows.
The format. Capture the second half in the same format as the first: a narrated slide deck, or a short video walkthrough of reference images arranged the way your scenes would be, with captions. The grade is for the pipeline and the thinking.

3
Brainstorm the sound plan
Ambience first
●
For each space in your work, name one sound that belongs to that place. That is your ambience track. Find it, or make it, before you reach for a score or effect that is merely decorative. A space that sounds like nowhere breaks the illusion faster than a space that looks slightly wrong.
For each space, one sound that really belongs there
Then say where each sound will come from: a royalty-free library, or recorded or made by you.
4
Finish the capture, then add titles and citations
The whole piece, captured
●
Record the second half of your script or narration in script order, the same way you captured the first half in Milestone 10. When you finish, the whole piece is captured. Then build the supporting media: title cards for the piece and, where a scene needs one, an on-screen citations list naming your sources.
List the scenes or stations in your second half, in order
One line each. These finish the piece.

Your title cards and on-screen citations
Write the text of each title card. Then list the sources your on-screen citations will name.

Check your license notes
Flag, verify, keep or replace, log
●
Use Claude, then log it. Describe your sound plan and its license notes to Claude and ask it to flag anything that looks unlicensed, uncredited, or unclear. Treat every flag as a question, not a verdict. Check the asset's actual license page, then keep, replace, or remove the asset and record the outcome.
