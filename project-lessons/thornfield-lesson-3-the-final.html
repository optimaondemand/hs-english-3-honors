This week the Draft becomes the Final. You cut, reorder, and sharpen it, run the five-point edit, check every quotation and fact, and turn it in.
YOUR PATH TODAY
Revise in three passes→Edit with the checklist→Verify quotes and facts→Assemble and turn in
You will need:About 60 minutesYour Draft and teacher feedbackThe novel (gutenberg.org/ebooks/1260)
1
Revise: cut, reorder, sharpen
Three passes, in this order
●
The Final is the module’s main graded work, 50 points. It is your Draft made better, not a new piece. Make three passes (the revision lesson), and use your teacher’s feedback on the Draft as you go.
A MODEL, ON THE HOUSE-PARTY TOPIC
Before: In this paragraph I am going to explain how it was that in Victorian times a house party would have required a very large number of servants who worked.
After: A Victorian house party kept dozens of servants working around the clock.
QUICK CHECK · NOT GRADED
Which change is a cut?
What did your teacher’s feedback on the Draft ask you to change?
If you have no feedback yet, name the weakest part of your own Draft.
2
Your three passes
Say exactly what you changed
Cut: what did you remove, and why?
Reorder: what did you move, and why?
Sharpen: give one sentence before and after
3
Edit: the five-point checklist
The editing-checklist lesson, one point at a time
●
Editing comes after revising. Revising changes the ideas. Editing fixes the surface. Go through five points: concision, consistency of headings and tense, agreement, quotation punctuation, and a final MLA check.
1
Concision
No wasted words
2
Consistency
Headings and tense match
3
Agreement
Subjects and verbs agree
4
Quotations
Punctuation is exact
5
MLA
A last citation check
QUOTATION PUNCTUATION · A MODEL
"I am no bird; and no net ensnares me; I am a free human being with an independent will, which I now exert to leave you."
Jane Eyre, ch. 23. In your own text you would add the citation: (Brontë, ch. 23).
QUICK CHECK · NOT GRADED
Where does the period go when a quotation is followed by a parenthetical citation?
Type your headings in one line, separated by slashes
Read them aloud. Are they parallel?
What did the checklist catch in your piece?
Name at least two things you fixed.
4
Verify every quotation and fact
A feature a stranger can trust
●
Every fact you state about the real world (wages, law, publication history, literary habits) needs a source behind it. Every claim about the novel needs a citation by chapter. Check both before you submit. The novel is free on Project Gutenberg: gutenberg.org/ebooks/1260.
Retype your pull-quote now, checked against the text
Compare it letter by letter. Include the chapter.
List three facts about the real world in your feature and the source for each
List two claims about the novel and the chapter that proves each
●
Use Claude the narrow way: ask it to run the stranger test on a paragraph, flag a term you used without defining, or check whether a claim needs a source. Claude never drafts or words a sentence of your feature. Verify any source it names on JStor before you cite it. Claude may flag a problem; it may never write the fix.
Your AI-use log line for this revision
What you asked, what it said, what you kept. If you did not use Claude, say so.
