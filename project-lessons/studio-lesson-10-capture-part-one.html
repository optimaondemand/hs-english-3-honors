What this milestone is
The first half, captured in order
●
Milestone 9 built the spaces. Milestone 10 is where you start capturing them: working through your assembled scenes in the order your script follows, and recording the words that go with it. You capture only the first half this time. The rest is Milestone 11.
The format. Capture the first half as a narrated slide deck, or a short video walkthrough of reference images arranged the way your scenes would be, with captions. The grade is for the pipeline and the thinking.

See one capture-log entry
A model, and a check
WHAT A CAPTURE-LOG ENTRY LOOKS LIKE (A MODEL, NOT YOUR ANSWER)
Captured: Station 2, the printing office, narration for the newspaper-origin sequence.
What worked: framing from the doorway kept the press in frame the whole time, so the narration could point at it directly instead of describing it.
What the medium changed: a line written to be read silently had to be shortened for spoken delivery. A long phrase about a persuasive rather than a purely deliberative document became: meant to persuade, not just to decide.
Narration: recorded on a phone, one take, transcript attached.
Slide: slide 4 narrated over the same three arranged reference images.

4
Order the first half, then plan the moment
Your capture plan
●
Your first half is the scenes or stations that make up the first half of your script or narration, in the order your viewer will experience them. Write that order down. Then, for each one, name the single moment you most need the viewer to feel. Ask honestly: can your capture method deliver that moment? If the answer is no, adjust your approach, your pacing, or your shot before you start recording, not after.
List the scenes or stations in your first half, in order
One line each, with its number from Milestone 9.
For each scene or station, the one moment you most need the viewer to feel
Then answer: can my capture method deliver it? If not, what will I change?

Record narration or dialogue for that half on any device: phone, laptop, or tablet. Add captions or a written transcript so a teacher can follow it without playing the sound.
