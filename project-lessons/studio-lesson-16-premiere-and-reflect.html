What this milestone is
Premiere, speak, reflect
●
Your kit is published. This last milestone is the moment it is shown, and the moment you stand behind it in person. The premiere is a class viewing of your work inside Canvas, or a scheduled session with your teacher. At it, you deliver your narration or a key monologue live, from memory. If a live delivery is genuinely impossible, record it on video instead, eyes covered, the same way the Module 4 On-Air Recitation asks. After the premiere, you write a short year-end reflection.

Write the year-end reflection
About 400 words
WHAT A YEAR-END REFLECTION OPENING LOOKS LIKE (A MODEL, NOT YOUR ANSWER)
I set out to argue that Douglass's literacy was a collective act, not a solitary one. Framing the schoolroom at the boys' eye level did more of that arguing than any line of narration could. What surprised me was how much the medium argued against me in one place. Framing the shot that close made the scene feel intimate and private, the opposite of what I wanted, until I added the ambient sound of the street outside to keep the world present. If I built this again, I would establish that sound earlier, not fix it at the edit.
●
After your premiere, write your year-end reflection, about 400 words. Cover three things. What this project taught you about the unit's contested question. What the medium (a narrated slide deck or short video walkthrough) did to your argument that a written essay could not have done. One thing you would change if you built it again.
