The Draft
The Draft is your first complete episode. It is graded on completion, so a rough, honest, full version earns the points. Next week you revise it into the Final. Today you plan each segment here, then write the script in Word.
YOUR PATH TODAY
Plan your script→ Write the first two segments→ Write the case→ Write the close and mark it→ Record and attach→ Download your PDF
You will need:About 60 minutesYour PDF from The WorkshopWord, for the script itselfAny device that records sound
1
What the Draft is
One script, one recording, one log
●
This week's grade is for completing a genuine full script and a rough recording of the whole thing. Leave yourself something to improve. Your teacher will leave one or two comments to steer next week's revision.
QUICK CHECK · NOT GRADED
What earns full points on the Draft?
Your episode title
It must sound like something a listener would choose to press play on. Not "My Episode."
Your four segments and timings
Copy them from above, and adjust if you changed your mind. The total stays between 8 and 12 minutes.
2
Segment 1: the cold open
Your hook, in full
●
Take the strongest of your three hooks and write the cold open in full. A cold open only has one job: make someone want the next several minutes. Say the line the way you would if it mattered to you.
Write the cold open
Roughly 130 to 200 words. Type it here, then copy it into your Word script.
3
Segment 2: the recitation
The passage you speak from memory, eyes covered
●
The recitation is the one segment you do not write. It is the passage you learned. In your script, you give its source and note that you will record it eyes covered. You also write one line before it and one line after it so the listener knows it has started and ended. Do not type the passage itself into this lesson. It goes in your Word script and your own recording.
Your passage and its source
Title, author, and where it is in the work. If it comes from the novel, you copy it from your own copy.
Your line before the recitation
One sentence that tells the listener something important is about to be spoken.
Your line after the recitation
One sentence after a pause. It brings the listener back to your argument.
4
Segment 3: the case
Your argument, answering a real objection
●
The case is the longest segment and carries the most points on the Final. It must answer a real, verified objection, not an easy one. You already tested your idea in the Workshop. Build the case in four moves.
1
Open
Your opening line, in the shape you picked
2
Name the objection
State it fairly, so its supporters would agree
3
Answer it
Use what you verified
4
Land it
End on why the work must survive
Your opening line
The objection, stated fairly
Your answer
Name what is true in the objection, then show why it does not outweigh what would be lost. Use only what you checked.
The line that lands
The last thing the listener hears before the close.
5
Segment 4: the close
Sign-off, credits, sources read aloud
●
The close tells the listener where your case came from, so someone without your show notes still knows. What you say must match your source list.
Write the close
Sign-off, your credits, and your sources named aloud, briefly.
6
Mark your script for the ear
Breath, stress, pauses, and transitions
●
A script that reads well can fall flat when spoken. Marks remind you what you decided in rehearsal. A single slash is a light breath. A double slash is a fuller stop. Bold is the one stressed word in a sentence. Brackets hold a pace or pause note. Between segments, add one line that says what ended and what begins.
A MARKED EXCERPT, WRITTEN FOR THIS LESSON
Today, / the library closes. // [slow down] It was open for ninety years. / It was the only room in town / where no one asked you to buy anything. [pause, two seconds] // Tonight, / we keep that room alive. [pick the pace back up]
QUICK CHECK · NOT GRADED
In the excerpt, what does the double slash tell the speaker?
Mark the first three sentences of your cold open
Copy them here and add breath marks, one stressed word per sentence, and one deliberate pause.
Two sentences: what changed once you marked it, and what did you notice only when you read it aloud?
Your three transition lines
One line each: cold open to recitation, recitation to case, case to close.
7
Record a rough read-through
Unedited is completely fine this week
●
Record the whole episode, start to finish, on any device you have. Speaking it aloud once is itself a form of editing. Any music or sound must be royalty-free or made by you, and credited. Silence is a fine choice too.
BEFORE YOU PRESS RECORD
  • Find a quiet room and read your marked script aloud once, as practice.
  • Record all four segments in order without stopping. Keep going through mistakes.
  • Save the recording as a file, or save it where you can share a link, such as OneDrive.
  • Write your script in Word and save it as LastName_FirstName_LivingBooks_ELA11_Draft1 in OneDrive: Optima → ELA → ELA11.
