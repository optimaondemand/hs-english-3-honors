1
Listen first, then revise
A recording teaches what a script cannot
●
A page rewards a reader who can slow down and reread. A listener gets one pass at the pace you set. A sentence built for the page can run out of breath when spoken. So play your rough recording and follow along on your script. Mark every place you stumbled or got lost. Then make three moves.
MOVE 1 · CUT THE WRITTEN-ONLY SENTENCE
A sentence that works only because you can see its punctuation often collapses aloud. If you stumble on it twice, do not reword it. Cut it, or split it into two short spoken sentences.
MOVE 2 · MOVE THE STRONGEST LINE EARLIER
A reader can skim ahead. A listener just stops. If your best line sits three minutes in, move it, or an echo of it, into the first thirty seconds.
MOVE 3 · LET THE RECITATION BREATHE
The moment you stop arguing and speak the passage is the center of the episode. Leave a real pause before it and a real pause after it, in the script and in the recording.

The final editing checklist
Five points, for your script and your show notes together
●
A listener will never see this checklist, and that is the point. Small slips in a name, a citation, or a caption are what keep the argument from doing its work. Run all five points before your last take.
1
Names and titles
Spelled and capitalized the same way everywhere

Draft your show notes
The page a listener reads beside your episode
●
Show notes are saved as a Word document with four parts: a short summary, a segment list with timings, an MLA source list with at least two credible sources including one from JStor, and credits for any music or sound and its license. Draft each part here, then paste it into Word.
Summary
Two or three sentences that tell a stranger what the episode saves and why.
Segment list with timings
Update the timings to match your final recording.
MLA source list
At least two credible sources, one from JStor. Alphabetize them.
Credits
Music or sound, and its license. Write "none" if you use only your voice.
Which claims in your case did you verify, and against what?
Every factual claim must be checked before you submit.
