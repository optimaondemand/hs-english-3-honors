2
The records you keep
Four things to know before you start
●
Three records follow your work all year, and you will meet each one in these lessons. Your Source and Line Log lists every source your work uses and every line or fact that comes from it, so any claim can be traced back. Your AI-use log gets one line per milestone: what you asked an AI tool, what it flagged, what you checked, and what you changed. Your teacher reads them to see how you worked. You also write a short log entry in each milestone to show what you built or captured.

3
What your teacher grades: the Master Rubric
Four bands, 25 points each
25of 100
CRAFT
Film grammar (framing, sequence, shot choice), sound, pacing, captions. Deliberate choices, named correctly in your statement.
25of 100
ACCURACY
Every reference or claim traces to a logged source. Both reviews documented. Departures disclosed.
25of 100
ARGUMENT
Your director's statement poses the contested question, takes a position, and answers the strongest alternative reading.
25of 100
RESEARCH
At least four credible sources, at least two from JStor, each annotated for how it was used and verified.
●
Each milestone is worth 15 points and is graded on completion: is the work there, is it honest, is it logged. Your finished kit, delivered in Quarter 4, is graded once on these four bands. Your live delivery at the premiere is scored separately on the 20-point recitation rubric (accuracy, delivery, interpretation) and counts as your Quarter 4 recitation. The Master Rubric page, linked in your Module 3 Week 1 materials, has the full descriptions. Read it once before you build.

Set up your studio
Your mode, and the format you build in
●
Production starts with a list. Your Design Package from Milestone 8 describes the spaces your work needs: scenes if you are adapting a dramatic work (the dramatic track), stations if you are making a documentary (the documentary track). A station is one stop in a documentary walkthrough. Reread it now. Your mode, locked in Milestone 2, tells you which track you are on.

Which format are you building in?

The format. Build each space as a narrated slide deck, or a short video walkthrough of reference images arranged the way your scenes would be, with captions. The grade is for the pipeline and the thinking.

See one space before you build it
A plan, a model entry, a check
●
Before you assemble anything, picture the space from above. This plan shows one station from the model below. The camera sits where the dot is. The dashed line is the first thing it frames. Decide those two things for every space and the rest of the build follows.
WHAT A BUILD-LOG ENTRY LOOKS LIKE (A MODEL, NOT YOUR ANSWER)
Space: Station 2, the printing office (documentary track, the 1787 ratification debate).
Placed: a hand press, stacked broadsheets, one lamp, a single window with evening light. The shot is framed from the doorway so the press is the first thing seen.
Why: the essays were written for newspapers, so the room where they were set in type carries the argument that this was persuasion, not decree.
Sources: the newspaper origin of the Federalist essays (Module 2); the press design checked against a museum photograph, Source and Line Log entry 14.
Slide: slide 4, three reference images arranged left to right as the scene would be seen.

6
List your spaces
One line each. This is your build plan.
●
For each space in your Design Package, write one sentence: what is the first thing the viewer should see, and what should they feel before a word is spoken? That sentence is your build target. If you cannot write it, the space is not designed yet. Go back to your Design Package before you open the tools.
List every space your script or narration needs. Number each one, name it, and give it a one-line build target.
Example: 1. The schoolroom. First thing seen: the slate with the alphabet. Feeling: small and curious.

8
Check one space for anachronisms
Flag, verify, correct or keep, log
●
Use Claude, then log it. Describe one finished space to Claude in words and ask it to flag anything that might be anachronistic or inaccurate for your unit's period. An anachronism is a detail from the wrong time, like a modern bulb in an 1800s room. Treat every flag as a question, not a verdict. Claude guesses from patterns, so it can flag something true and miss something false. You verify each flag against a real source.
Describe one finished space in words
Objects, materials, light, and any clothing. Words only. Write the description you will give Claude.

Name the source you opened. Claude points. You decide.
