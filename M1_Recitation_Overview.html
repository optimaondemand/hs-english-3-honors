THE MODULE 1 RECITATION
Choose a Passage, Learn It by Heart, and Speak It
Read this page in Week 1 so you can choose early and rehearse all module. You will record and submit your recitation in Week 7.
🎤 WHAT THIS IS

Once each quarter you memorize and perform a passage from that quarter's text. This year-long thread builds to a final performance in Quarter 4. Memorizing a great speech is not busywork: when the words are inside you, you understand them differently, and you carry them for life. This quarter your passage comes from Hamlet.

🎯 HOW YOU PERFORM IT (READ CAREFULLY)

You recite from memory, with your eyes covered. Wear a blindfold, close your eyes, or turn away from any screen or page. This proves the words are truly memorized and frees you to perform rather than read. There are two ways to do it, and you choose one:

🎥 Option A: Record a video
Record yourself reciting with eyes covered, then submit the video on the Week 7 assignment page. Include a caption or transcript so it can be reviewed without a headset.
💬 Option B: Live with your facilitator
Your facilitator may instead choose to meet with you and hear the recitation live, also with eyes closed. If so, they will schedule it; you do not submit a video.
📜 CHOOSE ONE PASSAGE (15–25 LINES) · TAP TO PREVIEW

Each choice below is a continuous passage of about 15 to 25 lines. Pick the one whose voice you most want to inhabit. Read the whole speech in context before you decide. Public-domain text with line numbers: Folger Shakespeare, Hamlet.

Hamlet's first soliloquy · Act 1, scene 2
From "O, that this too too solid flesh would melt" to "But break, my heart, for I must hold my tongue." (about 25 lines)

Grief, disgust at his mother's hasty marriage, and the first crack of the play's darkness. Choose this if you want raw, spoken feeling. Watch the shifts from mourning to revulsion to forced silence.

"To be, or not to be" · Act 3, scene 1
From "To be, or not to be, that is the question" to "And lose the name of action." (about 20 lines; recite this continuous opening portion)

The most famous speech in English. Choose it only if you can make it your own and not a recital of a quotation. It rewards slow, thinking delivery over drama.

"O, what a rogue and peasant slave am I" · Act 2, scene 2
From "O, what a rogue and peasant slave am I!" to "The play's the thing / Wherein I'll catch the conscience of the king." (recite a continuous 18–24 line span ending on the couplet)

Self-disgust that turns into a plan. Choose this for range: it moves from shame to fury to a sudden, cold idea. Great for a performer who likes a build.

"How all occasions do inform against me" · Act 4, scene 4
From "How all occasions do inform against me" to "My thoughts be bloody, or be nothing worth!" (about 20 lines; note: fuller in some editions)

Hamlet shamed into resolve by watching an army march to die for "an eggshell." Choose this if you want the turn toward action, and a strong closing line to land.

Advice to the players · Act 3, scene 2 (prose)
From "Speak the speech, I pray you, as I pronounced it to you" to "the censure of the which one must in your allowance o'erweigh a whole theatre of others." (about 18–22 lines of prose)

Not verse but prose, and secretly about how to perform well: truthful, proportioned, never overdone. A witty, teacherly choice, and a fitting one for a recitation.

If you are unsure, read all five aloud once and pick the one your voice returns to.
📊 HOW IT IS GRADED (20 POINTS)
Criteria Strong Developing Pts
Accuracy of text
7 pts		 From memory, eyes covered, faithful to the words; slips are rare and self-recovered. Frequent errors or long stalls, or eyes open / clearly reading. 7
Vocal delivery
7 pts		 Clear and controlled; pace, pause, and stress serve the sense. Rushed or flat; pauses fall mechanically at line-ends; hard to follow. 7
Interpretation
6 pts		 Delivery choices show real understanding of the passage's meaning and mood. Recited correctly but with little sense of what the words mean. 6
Total: 20 pts
