📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A broadcast has two documents, not one: the thing a listener hears, and the thing a reader checks. Both need a final pass.
Before an episode goes out, run one last check on both halves of it: the script to be recorded, and the show notes that go with it. This is a final check, not new grammar; every convention below has come up already this year. No new Word Study this week, because the module's words were reviewed in the previous week's final grammar lesson. Instead, this lesson is entirely about the habits that keep a finished piece of work from tripping over something small right before it goes out.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to run a five-point checklist against my own script and show notes before the work goes out.
2
I will be able to check my MLA citations and my caption or transcript for consistency with what is actually spoken.
3
I will be able to use every module word correctly, on demand, one last time.
✅ PART 1 · THE FINAL EDITING CHECKLIST

Run this against your script and your show notes together, in order, before recording the last take.

1. Consistency of names and titles
Every name, title, and character is spelled and capitalized the same way every time it appears, in the script and in the show notes. A listener will not notice a small misspelling; a reader of your show notes will.
2. MLA in the show notes
Every source you used, including your JStor source, has a correct, complete MLA entry, alphabetized and hanging-indented. Every source cited in the script is on the list; nothing is on the list that the episode never actually used.
3. The source list read aloud in the close
Your close names your sources aloud, briefly, so a listener with no access to the show notes still knows where your case came from. Check that what you say aloud matches what the show notes list; do not let the two documents drift apart.
4. Captions or transcript for the preserved passage
The passage segment inside the episode, and any separate recording of it, both need a caption or transcript with the exact passage and its citation. Check the two match each other exactly.
5. File naming
Show notes and episode audio are named in the pattern LastName_FirstName followed by what the file is, and both are saved before either one goes out.

💪 CARRY THIS FORWARD

Nobody who hears an episode will ever see its checklist. That is exactly the point: the small, unglamorous work of checking a spelling, a citation, a caption against a recording is what lets the argument and the voice do the work they are actually meant to do.
